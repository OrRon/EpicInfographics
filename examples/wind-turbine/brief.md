# Brief — wind-turbine

- **Style:** cutaway · **Canvas preset:** a4 (1240×1754)
- **Brief:** "how a wind turbine works — slice it open like a DK cross-section"
- **Data** (typical utility-scale onshore turbine, rounded — placard note: "typical 4 MW onshore machine — figures rounded"):
  - Rated power ≈ 4 MW · powers ≈ 3,000 homes at EU-average use
  - Hub height ≈ 100 m · rotor diameter ≈ 130 m · each blade ≈ 63 m
  - Rotor turns ~15 rpm; the gearbox steps it up ~100× to ~1,500 rpm for the generator
  - Blade tips travel up to ~290 km/h
  - Cuts in at ~11 km/h wind, shuts down for safety at ~90 km/h

Use ONLY this data; rephrasing allowed, no new statistics.

## Audience & goal
General/curious readers (a museum-wall explainer); goal is to inform —
"the box at the top is a gearbox and a generator, and the numbers are wild."

## Story
One sentence: *a slow 15 rpm windmill hides a 100× gearbox that turns it
into a 1,500 rpm power plant for 3,000 homes.* Hero = the sliced-open
nacelle; the 15 → 1,500 rpm step-up is the loudest fact, staged in the
gearbox detail lens.

## Metaphor
The machine IS the diagram: the nacelle is cut open (magnified ~10×) with
its drivetrain labeled 1–6; a to-scale elevation inset carries the true
geometry (100 m hub, 130 m rotor, a human dot for scale); homes-powered is
an icon army; the cut tower reveals the ladder a technician actually climbs.

## Composition
Big Object (cutaway's native pattern). Dense zone: the nacelle interior
and its callouts; empty zone: open paper left of the tower. Boundary
crossing: the upper blade bleeds off the top edge. Detail lens on the
gearbox with the 15 → 1,500 rpm step-up.
