# Brief — visit-mars

- **Style:** park-poster · **Canvas preset:** story (1080×1920)
- **Brief:** "a WPA-style national park poster that sells a trip to Mars with real numbers"
- **Data** (NASA Mars fact sheet figures, rounded — footer note: "figures: NASA Mars fact sheet"):
  - Olympus Mons 21.9 km high — about 2.5× Everest (8.8 km)
  - Valles Marineris canyon: ~4,000 km long, up to 7 km deep
  - Gravity 0.38 g · a sol lasts 24 h 39 m · average temperature −63 °C
  - Average Earth–Mars distance ≈ 225M km · transfer time with current rockets ≈ 7 months
  - Two moons: Phobos and Deimos

Use ONLY this data; rephrasing allowed, no new statistics.

## Audience & goal
General public, space-curious; goal is to impress and delight (poster-first,
data woven in). Lives on a phone screen — story preset.

## Story
One sentence: *the tallest mountain in the solar system is one 7-month trip
away — and Everest only reaches a third of the way up it.* Hero = the
comparative skyline (Olympus Mons vs Everest plotted to true relative
height). Support: the journey arc from Earth, the visitor-conditions stat
row, Valles Marineris in the foreground ridge.

## Metaphor
The vista IS the chart: mountain silhouettes are drawn at true relative
heights (a comparative elevation skyline), the Earth→Mars transfer is a
dashed trajectory in the sky, and the canyon is a notch cut through the
foreground silhouette. Reader is standing on a Martian overlook.

## Composition
Big Object vista in layered planes (banded sky → sun disc → far haze →
Olympus massif → mid ridge → ink foreground). Dense zone: skyline + stat
strip; empty zone: upper-left sky. Boundary crossings: the Olympus slope
and the Valles Marineris rift both run off the right frame edge, and the
transfer arc descends behind the massif.
